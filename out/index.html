Second Window

Thousands of clinicians tried JotPsych and did not subscribe. Most did not say no — the timing was wrong. Second Window watches the federal NPI registry for the practice changes that reopen that decision, writes to those clinicians at that moment, and stays quiet the rest of the time.

The checks, and why each one exists

Surveillance

The registry decides when to write. Saying so out loud turns a well-timed message into being watched — and a clinician who feels watched never comes back.

Unearned claim

The identity match was not confident enough to name this fact. If it is the wrong person, we just wrote to a stranger about someone else's practice.

Signal leak

Quoting the value that changed proves we were watching, even without admitting it.

Forbidden claim

Something JotPsych cannot say — a reimbursement guarantee, or a certification that does not exist.

Reads as AI

Phrasing that tells a busy clinician nobody read this before it was sent.

Patient data

Behavioural health. Nothing resembling patient information may ever appear in outbound.

Judge

The LLM judge read the draft against the fact pack and the voice spec and rejected it, quoting the offending text.

Template artifact

An unfilled placeholder survived into the draft.

Malformed

The drafter returned nothing usable, or the draft is the wrong length for a clinician to read.

Nothing here is delivered to a clinician. Approved messages wait for a person; the only message that leaves on its own is a report to whoever operates the workspace. Writing to clinicians is a separate switch a company turns on once, deliberately.

This workspace runs on sample data. Clinician names, emails and mobile numbers are invented; registry records are real public NPPES data for same-named clinicians and are used only to exercise identity matching. No real clinician is a JotPsych user and none has been contacted.
